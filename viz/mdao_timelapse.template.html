Coupling beliefs — assumed vs published

Distributions over the jointly trusted box, each curve normalized to its own peak. Dashed = the consumer's belief about its partner (a vertical line is a scalar assumption); filled = what the supplier actually publishes. Consistency is checked design-by-design, not by curve overlap — the red strip on the baseline marks where designs fail the pointwise tolerance — the same red as a REPUBLISH verdict, because that is what it is: designs whose premises do not hold — which similar-looking silhouettes can hide. Separation is staleness.

How to read this

Each agent publishes a region — constraint margins over the shared design vector, conditioned on assumed coupling values. Colored contours are each discipline's feasibility boundary on a 2-D slice (AR × W₀) taken through the corner design — so the ringed aircraft always sits inside the region it annotates. Hatching marks overlap built on stale assumptions; the glowing region survives both the margin check and the fixed-point check. Dots are full 6-D samples classed on their own values, so a dot can still disagree with the slice beneath it — it has its own S, t/c, sweep and power. Admissible designs earn a planform, drawn to scale from their own variables. Rounds now learn: the dashed box is the trust region the next round samples, faint gold dots are the archive carried in from earlier rounds, and the trace below separates yield (how good the search is) from volume (how big the answer is). Yield climbing while volume stays flat is the search getting smarter — not the design space improving.
